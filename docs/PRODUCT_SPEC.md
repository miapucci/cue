# Cue — Product Spec (Psychology & Strategy)

**One sentence:** Cue is the mobile marketplace where creators post a brief, talent films it from home, and great content gets made in 48 hours.

---

## 1. Business Model Psychology

### Pricing architecture (Decoy + Anchoring)

| Tier        | Price    | Psychology                                      |
|------------|----------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Free       | $0       | Hook with 1 free brief                           |
| Creator    | $19/mo   | **Target** — feels cheap next to Pro              |
| Creator Pro| $49/mo   | Decoy that makes Creator feel like a steal       |

- Pro tier exists so Creator is the obvious choice. Research: decoy tier → ~47% higher mid-tier conversion, 31–74% ARPU lift.
- **Frame annual as monthly:** Show “$15.99/month, billed annually,” never “$228/year” (Headspace-style).

### Endowment effect (conversion engine)

- Let creators **post their first brief for free**. After they write the brief, set the vibe, and see talent profiles, they’ve already “claimed” the product.
- Convert by protecting what they have, not by gaining something new.

### Loss aversion at paywall

- ❌ “Upgrade to contact talent”
- ✅ “You have 3 talent submissions waiting. Upgrade to view them.”
- Research: loss-framed copy ~21% better conversion than gain-focused.

### Talent side

- **100% free to join.**
- **$9/mo “Boost”** — profile at top of brief searches. Frame: “Talent with Boost earn 3x more bookings on average.” Separate revenue that scales with supply.

## 2. Aesthetics / Design Psychology

- **Dark mode as default** (primary experience). Gen Z skews dark; signals premium and reduces eye strain.
- **Dopamine design:** Bold accent colors only on **reward moments** (CTAs, earnings, approvals). Overuse kills the hit.
- **8-second rule:** One clear primary action per screen. Gen Z drops confusing apps in under 8 seconds.
- **Variable reward:** Brief browsing = swipeable feed (cards), not search/filter. Every swipe might reveal a great brief.

---

## 3. Feature Psychology / Gamification

### Research warning

- Heavy gamification → higher week-1 engagement but **~67% abandonment by week 4** (vs ~38% non-gamified). Extrinsic motivation fades.
- **Simple gamification** (streaks, counters, completion) gives most of the benefit with less complexity. Focus on **visible progress toward real outcomes** (money, reels, recognition).

### For talent

- **Earnings dashboard as home** — running total; number goes up → more opens.
- “Appeared in X videos” on profile — social proof + achievement, no badges needed.
- **Profile completion bar** (Zeigarnik) — e.g. “Add a sample clip to unlock 3x more brief invitations.”
- **“Rising Talent”** badge — accounts &lt;30 days with ≥1 booking (cold-start + identity).
- **Response time** shown (“Responds in &lt; 2 hours”) — reward fast replies, don’t punish slow.

### For creators

- **“Your brief expires in 4 hours”** push — loss aversion for opens.
- **Live submission count** — “6 people have submitted for your brief” (social proof + urgency).
- **Creator streak** — e.g. post a brief and get content every week for 4 weeks → “Consistent Creator” (soft loop, no gambling).

### What NOT to do

- **Don’t combine badges and cash** for the same action (monetary reward gets diluted).
- **No leaderboards early** — discourages new talent.
- **No “late penalty”** for non-response — reward fast responders with visibility instead.

## 4. Trust Psychology

- **Verified video portfolio** — 3 sample videos **mandatory** before claiming briefs. Reel > star rating.
- **Mutual review** — both sides review after each transaction (Peerspace-style “force-enabled” trust).
- **Payment escrow** — creator pays upfront; funds held until they approve the video. Reduces creator risk and signals platform as trusted third party.
- **Show real, not perfect** — 4.5★ converts better than 5.0; emphasize authentic, detailed reviews over raw averages.
- **“Completed Videos” + “0 disputes”** — volume + reliability beats star count for many users.
- **Gen Z social proof** — reviews are core to the product, not optional.

### Cold start (new talent)

- **2 free “reduced rate” brief slots** — talent earns 50% of posting rate, no competition from established talent. Creators get a discount; talent gets first reviews. After **3 completed reviews**, talent enters the regular pool.

## 5. Gig Worker Psychology (Talent Retention)

### Three stages

1. **Attraction** — excitement, low barriers.
2. **Newbie challenge** — confusion, first rejection, “how do I get booked?”
3. **Empowerment** — pattern recognition, selective gigs, identity as “creator for hire.”

**Goal:** Get talent to stage 3 fast. Churn happens in stage 2.

### Beat stage 2 churn

- **Earnings calculator in onboarding** — e.g. “Talent who complete 4 briefs/month earn ~$240 on average.”
- **First brief feels guaranteed** — show briefs that match their profile before the full feed.
- **In-app guidance** — e.g. “Your profile is missing a sample clip — this is why you haven’t been booked yet.” Concrete, not shaming.

### Autonomy over money

- Gig workers cite **flexibility and “no boss”** above pay. Market talent side as: “Film what you want, when you want, from your room, and get paid.” Not “earn $300/month.” Intrinsically motivated talent tends to be more reliable and higher quality.
